Build notes

Sizing

40 A is continuous, so the circuit must be rated 125% of that: the existing 50 A GFCI 14-50 circuit qualifies. Every device in the 40 A path (cord, terminals, meter, contactor, J1772 cable) is rated 48 A or more. The 14-50 neutral is not used; land it on its own terminal and never bond it to PE inside the unit.

Pilot setup

Set the EVSE maximum current to 40 A in EVerest. That gives a 66.7% CP duty cycle. The ChargeBridge module generates the ±12 V pilot and runs the A–F state machine; the cable connector J11 has no ground pin, so CP returns through the single 0 V–PE bond. If the tethered cable has a fifth wire from the handle latch button, land it on PP (the module supports CCS1 attached-cable button detection); otherwise leave PP open. Keep total CP capacitance under 1600 pF and do not add an external CP clamp diode.

Contactor

Relay driver 1 closes K1 only in state C. The NC aux contact goes to feedback 1 so the module can refuse to start, or fault, if K1 welds. Measure the coil’s pull-in current against the driver rating in the datasheet before power-up.

Emergency stop

S1 is one red E-stop button on the right wall with two NC contact blocks, S1-a and S1-b. S1-a sits in K1's coil circuit, so pressing it opens K1 without relying on firmware. S1-b opens the loop to J4.11, so the module records the emergency and latches it until unplug. EMG IN is pulled up to 24 V through 100 kΩ on the board, so a bare NC contact would never trip it. R1 and two parallel pull-downs, R2a and R2b, make an open loop pull J4.11 low, and either pull-down alone is enough. The contacts are positive-opening, so they open even if welded. Repeat the step 10 S1 and S3 tests after moving the unit and every few months. To stop normally, use S2 or EVerest; avoid pulling the 14-50 plug under a 40 A load.

Ground-fault protection

Two layers. The 14-50 circuit's GFCI breaker trips at about 5 mA AC. RCM1, an RCM14-04 on the J1772 cable's L1 and L2, trips at 20 mA AC or 56 mA DC (the UL 2231 CCID20 levels), so the unit still has protection on an outlet without a GFCI. RCM1 acts through K2 and the E-stop loop: the module latches an emergency and opens K1. It isn't a certified CCID, and there's no ground-continuity monitor, so use the step 10 S3 test before relying on it at an outlet without a GFCI.

PT1000 front end

The bridge and INA333 (gain 30.1) reach the ADC's 2.048 V full scale at about 126 °C, so set terminal alarms well below that. 0.1 % resistor tolerance alone gives up to ±1.1 °C error without calibration. INA333 offset and gain error aren't modelled.

Not simulated: the K1 coil drive. TI publishes a PSpice model of the TPS27S100 (SLVMC96A), but the ESB63's electronic AC/DC coil isn't a plain inductor, so measure its inrush on the bench instead.

Assembly

Drawings are to scale at 1 mm per unit, looking into the enclosure base with the cover open. Part outlines come from manufacturer datasheets. Screw positions on K1, the aux contact, the meter, PS2 and K2 are approximate, so check them against the labels on your parts. Parts and wires added in each step are drawn in colour; earlier ones are grey.

Step 1: Prepare the enclosure

  1. Hold a DR-62 rail across the base on the mounting bosses. Rail centre lines are drawn 150 mm apart, at 95 mm and 245 mm from the top inside wall. Check that the ZH-161407 base has bosses for both rails before drilling anything. If it only has centred bosses, use the ZX-1614K internal panel and screw both rails to it.
  2. Drill the bottom wall for the glands: two M40 holes (about 40.5 mm) and one M20 hole (about 20.5 mm). Put the centres 40 mm out from the base and at 85 mm, 226 mm and 350 mm from the left inside wall. The M40 lock nuts are 50 mm across, so keep the centres at least 55 mm apart.
  3. Drill four 22.5 mm holes in the right wall, all 100 mm out from the base. Measured from the top inside wall: H1 (pilot light) at 40 mm, S2 (stop) at 95 mm, S1 (E-stop) at 175 mm, and S3 (RCM1 test) at 245 mm. Each needs 40–45 mm behind the wall; S1 needs 45 mm with its two contact blocks side by side. Mirror them onto the left wall if that's the side that will face you.
  4. Fit the glands, H1, S2, S1 and S3, then fit both rails. Clip S1's second NC block (ECX1030) beside the first, not behind it. The drill sizes are the usual thread size plus 0.5 mm; Bimed doesn't publish a clearance hole size.

Step 2: Fit the lower (power) rail

  1. Snap parts onto the lower rail from left to right: an end clamp, three UT 16-PE terminals (PE1–PE3), five UT 16 terminals (TB1-1 to TB1-5), then an end cover on the open side of TB1-5.
  2. Plug one FBS 2-12 bridge into TB1-1/TB1-2 and another into TB1-3/TB1-4. TB1-5 (N) stays unbridged and isolated.
  3. Leave a gap of about 11 mm, then snap on M1, the SDM230 meter.
  4. Clip the EH04-11N aux contact onto the left side of K1, then snap K1 on with the aux on the left, followed by an end clamp.
  5. Leave the next 30 mm of rail clear for wiring. Then fit an end clamp, PS2 (the DDR-15G-12, input terminals at the bottom), K2 (the Finder 38.51 relay) and a final end clamp. The rail is used up to about 337 of its 375 mm.

Step 3: Fit the upper (control) rail

  1. Screw the CB-EVAL board into the UM-PRO housing base with the board's J4/J5 edge toward the top. Check which way Phoenix's drawing puts the 120 mm board side relative to the rail; it's drawn here with 120 mm along the rail. Cut the right side element for the RJ45 jack, and the left one for the J10 24 V jack, which you'll need in step 10.
  2. Snap parts onto the upper rail from left to right: an end clamp, F1/F2 (the 2-pole FAZ), PS1, the board housing, then an end clamp.
  3. Switch F1/F2 off and leave it off until step 11.

Step 4: Wire the power path (8 AWG)

  1. Pass the 14-50 cord through the left M40 gland. Land G on PE1, L1 on TB1-1, L2 on TB1-3 and N on TB1-5, all on the bottom screws. N stops at TB1-5 and is never connected to PE.
  2. Run an 8 AWG green jumper from the top of PE1 to the top of PE2.
  3. Run 8 AWG black from TB1-1 (top) to M1 terminal 1, and from M1 terminal 2 to K1 terminal 1. Run 8 AWG red from TB1-3 (top) to K1 terminal 3. L2 doesn't pass through the meter.
  4. Pass the J1772 cable through the right M40 gland. Thread only its L1 and L2 through RCM1's 14 mm hole, then land L1 on K1 terminal 2 and L2 on K1 terminal 4. Route PE around RCM1, never through it, to PE2 (bottom). The pilot and sensor conductors also go around it.
  5. Fix RCM1 to the base just above the gland with two adhesive cable-tie mounts and ties through its flange holes, with its connector toward the right.
  6. Crimp ferrules on every stranded end and tighten each screw to the torque on its datasheet. Tighten the glands last, once the conductors are dressed.

Step 5: Wire the 24 V supply (18 AWG)

  1. Run 18 AWG black from TB1-2 (top) to F1/F2 terminal 1, and 18 AWG red from TB1-4 (top) to terminal 3. The bridges make these taps of L1 and L2.
  2. Run black from F1/F2 terminal 2 to PS1 L, and red from terminal 4 to PS1 N. PS1 has no earth terminal.
  3. Run a second red from F1/F2 terminal 4 to M1 terminal 3, the meter's voltage reference. This keeps the meter's sense lead behind the 2 A breaker.
  4. Run 18 AWG blue from PS1 +V to J7.11 and blue/white from PS1 −V to J7.12.
  5. Run 18 AWG green from PS1's second −V terminal to PE3 (top). This is the single 0 V–PE bond the control pilot needs.

Step 6: Wire the control signals

  1. Weld detection: run PS1's second +V terminal to aux 21, and aux 22 to J5.12. Put R3 (3.3 kΩ) across J5.12 and J5.9, sharing each screw with the wire in a twin ferrule.
  2. Meter: run the Belden 9841 pair from M1 terminals 5 (A) and 6 (B) to J4.8 (A) and J4.7 (B). Tie the shield to M1 terminal 7 at the meter end only.
  3. Pilot: run the J1772 cable's CP conductor to J11.6. If the cable has a latch-sense conductor, land it on J11.7. Keep these runs away from the 8 AWG conductors.
  4. Temperature: stick TT1 on K1's body beside terminals 2/4 and wire it to J11.2/J11.3. Stick TT2 on TB1-3's housing, the incoming L2 terminal, and wire it to J11.4/J11.5. Neither lead goes to ground.
  5. Stop button: wire S2's NO contact to J4.10 and J4.9.
  6. Not used: leave J11.8 (ZC) and the HV inputs J1/J6 unconnected.

Step 7: Wire the safety and status circuits

  1. Contactor coil through the E-stop: run J5.10 to S1-a (S1's upper contact block), S1-a on to K1 A1, and K1 A2 back to J5.9. Pressing S1 cuts the coil directly.
  2. E-stop loop: run a wire from PS1's second +V terminal (in a twin ferrule with the aux 21 feed) to S1-b, S1's lower contact block. Run S1-b's other side to K2 terminal 11, and K2 terminal 14 to R1 at J4.11. Fit R2a and R2b separately, each from J4.11 to J4.9. Put each terminal's leads and wire into one ferrule (at most 1.5 mm²). Keep R2a and R2b as two separate resistors; don't solder them together into one part, or a single bad joint could take out both.
  3. 12 V supply: run 18 AWG from PS1 +V (in a twin ferrule with the J7.11 wire) to PS2 +Vin, and from PS1 −V (in a twin ferrule with the J7.12 wire) to PS2 −Vin. Jumper PS2 −Vo to PS2 −Vin, so the 12 V side shares 0 V.
  4. RCM1 harness (JST XHP-4 housing with pre-crimped leads): pin 1 to PS2 −Vo (0 V), pin 2 to PS2 +Vo (+12 V), pin 3 to one side of S3, pin 4 to K2 A2. Run PS2 +Vo to K2 A1 too, observing the A1+ / A2− marking. In normal running RCM1 holds pin 4 low and K2 energised. A fault, a lost 12 V supply or a failed relay coil drops K2 and opens the E-stop loop.
  5. Test button: run S3's other side to PS2 +Vo. Pressing S3 puts 12 V on RCM1's test input, which simulates a fault.
  6. Pilot light: jumper aux 21 to aux 33, run aux 34 to H1 +, and H1 − to J5.5 (0 V). H1 lights whenever K1's main contacts are closed.

Step 8: Connect the PC and configure EVerest

  1. Pass the Ethernet cable through the M20 gland, then crimp the RJ45 plug and plug it into the board.
  2. On the PC, give the cable's interface a static address and run a DHCP server on it, for example dnsmasq --interface=eth1 --bind-interfaces --dhcp-range=192.168.50.100,192.168.50.150,12h. Find the module with avahi-browse _chargebridge._udp -tr.
  3. Start from EVerest's applications/pionix_chargebridge/config/config-CB-EVAL-SIM.yaml. Set the maximum current to 40 A, and under safety.relay_1 set feedback_enabled: true. Leave inverted_emergency_input: 0 (J4.11 low means emergency).
  4. Set up the SDM230 on /dev/cbserial0 at Modbus address 1, 9600 8N1, and set PT1000 limits for both channels, well below the front end's 126 °C full scale.

The example config ships with feedback_inverted: true. Confirm the polarity in step 10: feedback must report open while K1 is open. Flip the setting if it doesn't.

Step 9: Check it with the power off

  1. With the 14-50 plug out, check continuity from the plug's G pin to PE1, PE2, PE3, the J1772 PE pin and J7.12 (0 V, through the bond).
  2. Check that N isn't connected to PE, and that TB1-5 shows no continuity to L1, L2 or PE.
  3. Check that only L1 and L2 pass through RCM1's hole.
  4. Tug-test every conductor, and check that no copper shows outside a terminal or ferrule.
  5. With F1/F2 off and K1 open, megger L1 and L2 to PE at 500 V DC. Expect well over 1 MΩ. The meter is still connected, so keep the test short.

Step 10: Bring it up on 24 V only

  1. Keep the 14-50 unplugged and F1/F2 off. Plug the 24 V supply that came with the Pionix kit into J10. It back-feeds PS1's output terminals, so PS2, RCM1 and K2 come up too: K2 should click on.
  2. Check that the PC finds the module and that EVerest starts.
  3. Plug the J1772 handle into an EV simulator. In state B (2.74 kΩ + diode), the pilot should read +9 V and then show a 66.7% duty cycle. In state C (with 1.3 kΩ added), K1 should pull in with an audible click, H1 should light, feedback 1 should read closed, and TT1/TT2 should read room temperature.
  4. Press S2: K1 should drop out and H1 go dark. Pull the handle out: the session should end.
  5. Start a session, then press S1. K1 should drop out, and EVerest should report an emergency stop that stays latched until you unplug. Twist S1 to reset it, then unplug and replug the handle and confirm charging resumes.
  6. Start a session, then press S3. K2 should click off, K1 drop out, and EVerest latch an emergency. Release S3, then unplug and replug the handle.
  7. The S1 and S3 tests prove the E-stop loop, both pull-downs, K2 and RCM1's output all work. Repeat them after moving the unit and every few months, and before charging from an outlet that has no GFCI.
  8. Unplug the J10 supply.

Step 11: First run on 240 V

  1. Close the cover. Plug into the 14-50 and press the receptacle breaker's GFCI test button. It must trip; reset it.
  2. Switch F1/F2 on. PS1 should come up, then the board and EVerest, and K2 should click on.
  3. Repeat the simulator test. In state C, measure about 240 V between L1 and L2 at the simulator, and 0 V when it leaves state C.
  4. Charge a car. Watch K1 and TT1 temperatures and the meter's current for the first hour at 40 A.

Before energising. This is a bench test unit for private use, not a listed product. The ChargeBridge module is certified to its own standards; the assembly is not. Confirm the 14-50 receptacle's breaker trips on its test button, then bench-test the pilot and contactor at 24 V with a J1772 EV simulator before plugging into 240 V.
